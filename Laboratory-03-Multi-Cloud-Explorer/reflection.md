## 1. Which cloud platform impressed you the most?

The cloud platform that impressed me the most is Amazon Web Services (AWS). Among AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform (GCP), AWS stood out to me because of its wide range of services and its ability to support different types of organizations and workloads. During the activity, I learned that AWS provides services for computing, storage, databases, networking, security, containers, artificial intelligence, and many other areas of cloud computing. This showed me how a single cloud provider can provide many of the technologies that a business needs.

One thing that impressed me about AWS is Amazon EC2. EC2 allows users to create and run virtual servers in the cloud. This is especially useful because organizations do not always need to purchase and maintain their own physical servers. Instead, they can use cloud resources and adjust them depending on their requirements. AWS also provides Amazon S3 for object storage, Amazon RDS for managed relational databases, and AWS Identity and Access Management for controlling access to resources.

I also found AWS impressive because of its scalability. A business can start with a small amount of cloud resources and increase them as its users and applications grow. This is particularly helpful for startups and companies with changing workloads. AWS can also support large enterprise applications that require high availability and reliable infrastructure.

Overall, this laboratory helped me understand why AWS is widely used in cloud computing. I learned that choosing a cloud provider is not only about popularity but also about the services, scalability, flexibility, and requirements of an organization. AWS impressed me because it provides a broad collection of tools that can be used to build, deploy, manage, and scale many different types of applications.

## 2. What similarities exist among AWS, Azure, and GCP?

AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform (GCP) have many similarities because they are all major cloud computing providers that offer infrastructure and platform services to individuals, developers, businesses, and enterprises. Although their service names, interfaces, pricing structures, and specific features may differ, they all provide solutions for common cloud computing requirements.

One major similarity is that all three platforms provide computing services. AWS has Amazon EC2, Azure has Azure Virtual Machines, and GCP has Compute Engine. These services allow users to create virtual machines that can run operating systems such as Linux and Windows. This was related to the Linux investigation I performed using the KillerCoda Playground, where I examined an Ubuntu server and identified its operating system, CPU, memory, and disk space.

Another similarity is cloud storage. AWS provides Amazon S3, Azure provides Azure Blob Storage, and GCP provides Cloud Storage. These services allow organizations to store and manage data in the cloud. All three providers also offer database services, networking services, identity and access management, monitoring, security, and container technologies.

AWS, Azure, and GCP also support scalability. Businesses can increase or decrease their cloud resources depending on demand instead of depending completely on fixed physical infrastructure. They also provide services for containers and Kubernetes, allowing developers to deploy and manage modern applications.

Another important similarity is that all three platforms support enterprise workloads and provide global infrastructure. Organizations can choose regions and locations based on their application requirements and users.

Overall, AWS, Azure, and GCP have similar goals: they provide flexible, scalable, secure, and reliable cloud computing services. The main differences are in their individual strengths, service offerings, pricing, integrations, and technologies. Understanding these similarities makes it easier to compare the providers and select the platform that best matches a particular organization's requirements.

## 3. How do business requirements influence cloud platform selection?

Business requirements have a major influence on cloud platform selection because there is no single cloud provider that is automatically the best choice for every organization. A company needs to evaluate its specific goals, budget, existing technologies, applications, security requirements, performance expectations, and future plans before choosing a cloud platform. The decision should be based on what the organization needs rather than simply selecting the most popular provider.

For example, a startup company with a limited budget may prioritize affordable services, scalability, and ease of deployment. The startup may choose a provider that allows it to begin with small resources and increase them as the business grows. On the other hand, a university that already uses Windows Server, Microsoft 365, and Active Directory may benefit from Microsoft Azure because of its strong integration with Microsoft technologies.

Technology requirements can also affect the decision. An AI research company may prioritize artificial intelligence, machine learning, high-performance computing, and specialized computing resources. In this situation, the organization may consider GCP because of its strong AI and machine learning capabilities. A global e-commerce company, meanwhile, may prioritize high availability, automatic scaling, global infrastructure, and reliable networking because it needs to serve customers from different locations.

Security and compliance are also important business requirements. Organizations that handle sensitive information may need specific security controls, identity management features, monitoring tools, and compliance capabilities. Cost is another major consideration because cloud services can become expensive when resources are not properly managed.

Through this activity, I learned that cloud platform selection is similar to solving a business problem. A Cloud Solutions Consultant must first understand what the client needs and then match those needs with appropriate cloud services. The goal is not to choose AWS, Azure, or GCP simply because it is popular. The goal is to recommend a platform that provides the best combination of cost, performance, compatibility, scalability, security, and long-term value for the organization.
